Engagement

How a project runs.

01

A call, then one recommendation

The first call is to name the job, not to tour a catalogue. You leave with one recommendation: the system worth building first, and the ones that can wait.

02

Scope and price, in writing

The scope names the job, the limit, and the fee before any build starts. A chat can be short. A workflow takes longer. The duration is written down, not promised as a week for every kind of work.

03

Handover to the founder

Siddarth Boggarapu builds it and is the person you write to after. There is no shared inbox and no second login. The system lands in the tools you already open.

Who it is for

Owner-run businesses.

A clinic, a studio, a firm on WhatsApp. Not a company that already has a software team.

01

One job, named

A clinic, a studio, a shop, a small firm. The owner still answers the phone. If you cannot point at one task, reply, book, follow up, or draft, we do not start.

02

Tools you already open

The site, WhatsApp, Google Sheets, Gmail, and the calendar. The system is placed where the work already happens, so the team does not learn another product.

03

A person at the end

Siddarth Boggarapu and Ariv Shivakumar, in Bangalore. You write to Siddarth directly, at siddarthb078@gmail.com, not to a queue.

Handover

What you receive.

A running system. A short note on what it will refuse. A way to reach the founder.

01

The system, in place

A running system on your site, on WhatsApp, or in the mailbox you already send from. You can use it the week it ships, or it is not finished.

02

The boundary, in writing

A short note on what it may decide on its own, and what must wait for a person. That boundary is part of the delivery, not a footnote.

03

Time, quoted per job

Time is quoted for the job in front of us. We do not publish one turnaround for every engagement, because a receptionist and a workflow are not the same build.
